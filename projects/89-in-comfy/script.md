# 89
## S01









Six-seven is dead.

Let's talk eight-nine.

## S02









The 8th of May. And the 9th. Both mark the end of the war in Europe.

## S03









One long day here, in the former Wehrmacht engineers' school

## S04









in Berlin-Karlshorst, opened a fork that still runs across Europe.


And today it runs deeper than it has in decades.

## S05









So how did one delayed signature ceremony produce two legitimate dates?

## S06









To answer that question we have to go to Reims, France. On the 7th of May.

## S07









Here Colonel General Alfred Jodl signs the unconditional surrender of the Wehrmacht.

## S08









The guns are to fall silent at 23:01 on the 8th.

## S09









But Jodl is a staff officer with no real power of command.



And the British remember how the First World War ended

## S10









when German generals walked away from defeat.




So the Reims protocol demands a second signature.

## S11









From the men who actually give orders.

## S12









Both place and time were fixed: Soviet High Command headquarters in Karlshorst.

## S13









The 8th of May, afternoon.








But in order to secure an immediate ceasefire,

## S14









President Truman and Prime Minister Churchill announce the surrender before the ratification has happened.

## S15









While London and Washington begin celebrating, Karlshorst stalls.

## S16









The Russian translation arrives incomplete. Wording is argued over by radio.

## S17









The German High Command signs at about a quarter to one in the morning. The next day.

## S18









In the West the war had already been declared over. On the 8th.

## S19









In Moscow the announcement came only after Karlshorst.

## S20









May 9th — the date the Soviet Union took







as the victory over fascism in Europe and the end of the Great Patriotic War.

## S21









It is still in the official calendar in Russia, Armenia, Georgia, Kazakhstan and many other post-Soviet countries.

## S22









But that tradition has not held everywhere.






Latvia, Estonia and Lithuania dropped the 9th from the state calendar as soon as they regained independence.

## S23









Poland moved its commemoration to the 8th in 2015.





Ukraine did the same in 2023, and gave the 9th to Europe Day.

## S24









Thus the delay in Karlshorst contributed to today's disputes

## S25









and opened a way to choose the date without rewriting history.

## S26









Does that make remembering harder or freer?
