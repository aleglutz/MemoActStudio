# Signed After Midnight

**0:00** — A calendar can be a foreign policy instrument. In Europe, one date proves it every year.

**0:08** — The eighth and the ninth of May. Two dates for the end of the same war.  

**0:16** — How did one night in Berlin-Karlshorst produce two Victory Days?

**0:21** — Reims, France. The seventh of May. Colonel General Jodl signs the unconditional surrender of the Wehrmacht. The guns are to fall silent at 23:01 on the eighth.

**0:33** — But Jodl is a staff officer with no real power of command. And the British remember how the First World War ended — when German generals walked away from defeat.

**0:46** — So the Reims protocol demands a second signature. From the men who actually give orders.

**0:53** — The place is fixed: Soviet High Command headquarters in Berlin-Karlshorst. The eighth of May, afternoon.

**1:01** — But to secure an immediate ceasefire, President Truman and Prime Minister Churchill announce the surrender before the ratification has happened.

**1:09** — While London and Washington begin celebrating, Karlshorst stalls.

**1:14** — The Russian translation arrives incomplete. Wording is argued over by radio.

**1:20** — The German High Command signs at about a quarter to one in the morning. The next day.

**1:28** — In the West the war had already been declared over. On the eighth.

**1:35** — In Moscow the announcement came only after Karlshorst. May ninth — the date the Soviet Union took as the victory over fascism in Europe and the end of the Great Patriotic War.

**1:49** — It is still commemorated that way in Russia and across the post-Soviet space, including Armenia. But that tradition has not held everywhere.

**1:59** — Latvia, Estonia and Lithuania dropped the ninth from the state calendar as soon as they regained independence.

**2:07** — Poland moved its commemoration to the eighth in 2015. Ukraine did the same in 2023, and gave the ninth to Europe Day.

**2:17** — Thus the delay in Karlshorst contributed to today's disputes and opened a way to choose the date without rewriting history.

**2:25** — Does that make remembering harder — or freer?
