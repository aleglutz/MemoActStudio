# LEGENDS OF SURRENDER

### S00

The Second World War in Europe did not end at a single moment. That is where the legends come from. 

>-- white subtitles on black background

### S01

Nazi Germany signed the unconditional surrender twice — on May 7 in Reims, and on May 8 in Berlin-Karlshorst.

>-- frame stitched out of two, horizontal seam runs down the centre of the frame; the two halves slowly diverge upwards and downwards

### S02

As two acts of one surrender, together those events ended the war on every front.

>-- upper half with the date: **7 May 1945** [[Reims-Signing.jpg]] 
>-- lower half **Karlshorst** [[Karlshorst_Signing.jpg]] 

### S03

However, the memory of how the bloodiest war of the twentieth century came to an end in Europe was influenced by the Cold War, which began after 1945.

>-- transition from two-shots-frame to three-shots-frame

### S04

Each side cultivated its own view of the historic events. Three legends have been persistently retained up to the present time.

>-- **LEGENDS OF SURRENDER** appears in the middle section of a three-shots-frame as [[the-victors-copy.jpg]]

### S05

When the Soviets had to break silence about the first surrender, they tended to present it as a partial act. As if the German Wehrmacht surrendered only to the Western Allies on May 7 in Reims, and fully and unconditionally to the Soviet Armed Forces on May 8 in Karlshorst.

>-- title in the middle changed with: **LEGEND 1: German Wehrmacht surrendered only to the Western Allies on May 7 in Reims, and to the Soviet Armed Forces on May 8 in Karlshorst**
>-- two signing shots appear again that time colored: May 7 in Allied flags, May 8 in Soviet red

### S06

Despite the Nazi attempt to surrender only on the Western Front and continue maneuvers in the East, Eisenhower insisted on an immediate and unconditional surrender to all Allied troops.

>-- slow zoom-in on [[General_Dwight_D._Eisenhower.jpg]]

### S07

So the fact is that both acts took place before representatives of all the Allies — British, U.S. and Soviet, with the French as witnesses.

>-- two signing shots appear in black and white with coloring human figures in colors of the states they represented

### S08 

According to the second legend, Stalin did not recognize the Reims surrender and his official protest led to a second signing in Karlshorst. This account of events downplays the significance of the act in Berlin, emphasizing its ceremonial nature.

 >-- Stalin’s portrait appeared with the text in the middle: **LEGEND 2: Stalin did not recognize the Reims surrender** [[StalinCropped1943.jpg]]

### S09

But the second act was a Western demand. Particularly the British insisted that the signature of Jodl — an officer without the power of command — be reinforced by the signatures of those with the real power. 

Already in Reims the German delegation signed a supplementary protocol specifying ratification of the surrender by the commander in chief of the Wehrmacht and the supreme commanders of the three branches of the armed forces.

>-- zoom-in on [[Reims_Protocol_Jodl.jpg]]

### S10

On May 8 all those who participated met at Tempelhof airport, and were brought across the destroyed inner city to Karlshorst.

>-- archival video of arrival [[Tempelhof-arrival-VIDEO]]. TRY UPSCALE

### S11

The third legend puts a quarrel among the Allies inside that night. As if disputes among the Allies about the wording of the text led to a delay in signing until after midnight.

>-- one horizontal shot split transition to three-shots-frame with subtitle **LEGEND 3: Disagreement as a reason for the delay** [[Karlshorst-Prepared.jpg]]

### S12

In fact, the delay had technical reasons. In Reims only an English language document had been signed. The transmission of the Russian translation was initially incomplete.

>-- archival video of preparation with whispering to Zhukov’s eyer.

### S13

In the end, it was midnight before all preparations could be completed.

>-- zoom-in on the Russian text with the focus on date [[GIoS_Wehrmacht_Signed_Ru.jpg]]

### S14

On May 9 around 0.45 a.m. Field Marshal Keitel, General Admiral von Friedeburg and Colonel General Stumpff signed the surrender document, which was dated May 8.

### S16

According to Western European Time it was 23.45; according to Moscow time it was already two forty five the next day. 

>-- three dials arranged in a column, one below the other: 23.45 London, 00.45 Berlin, 02.45 Moscow with colorized shots of victory celebrations on a background.

### S17

May 9, the date on which the surrender was announced in Moscow, is considered there as marking the official end of the war in Europe.

>-- previous frame with 23.45, 00.45, 02.45.

### S18

The legend of a quarrel outlived the night that produced it, because the quarrel came later. Two calendars, two ceremonies, two victories. Every May the war in Europe still ends twice.

### S19

The official end of the war in Europe, however, did not mean the end of the suffering.

>-- black background, quote appears: „Everyone was very excited, except for us, the few refugees in the city. … Would I find any of my family alive at all?“  
> Moshe Beirach, Jewish partisan  

### S20

Armed conflicts continued in Eastern Europe well into the 1950s. The war claimed 60 million victims. The impact of World War II affects people and societies up to the present.
